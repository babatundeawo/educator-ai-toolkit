How it works

Three steps, then it's yours for the term

1

Set up once

Paste one instruction into a Claude Project and upload your e-notes. This takes about fifteen minutes and you never repeat it for that subject and class.

2

Ask in one sentence

Type something like "Generate Week 3, Physics, SS2" or "Third term exam for Basic Science, JSS1" whenever you need a document.

3

Download and print

Claude replies with a properly formatted Word file in under two minutes, ready to print, share, or edit further.

What you get

Two tools, built around how Nigerian schools actually run

70-mark exams, done fairly

30 objective questions plus 40 marks of theory, with a separate marking guide and randomly distributed answers every time.

Self-study revision files

Summary notes plus practice questions with the answer directly underneath, built entirely from that term's e-note.

Before you start

What you'll need

Device

A phone or a computer. Nothing else.

Connection

Internet, only while setting up or generating.

Materials

Your e-notes (all 3 terms), or a topic list if you don't have e-notes.

A note to supervisors and administrators

Why this strengthens documentation, rather than replacing the teacher

The document that comes out of either tool is a properly structured, curriculum-aligned exam, marking guide, revision file, or lesson note booklet, the same standard already expected of every teacher, produced faster and more consistently. The teacher still chooses the subject, class, term and week, still uploads or approves the source material, and still reviews and edits the result before it reaches a student. Claude removes the hours of manual formatting and typing; it does not remove the teacher's judgement.

Consistency

Every document follows the same header, font, margin and structure rules, term after term, teacher after teacher.

Traceability

Every exam and lesson note is generated from the teacher's own e-notes or topic lists, so content stays tied to what was actually taught.

Time saved

A weekly lesson note booklet or a 70-mark exam with marking guide is ready in about two minutes, instead of a full evening of typing.

The goal is simple: turn a task that used to take a whole evening of typing into one sentence and a two-minute wait.
