Where the topic and content come from

The Scheme of Work is authoritative for what topic is taught in a given week, for every subject and class. An uploaded e-note or textbook is a preferred source for the content of that topic, never the source of the topic itself. If a Subject has no entry anywhere in the scheme (e.g. Yoruba, Arabic), Claude falls back to an uploaded e-note or online research for that subject only.

Non-teaching weeks

If the requested week's Scheme of Work entry is a non-teaching label (Midterm Examination, Midterm Break, Revision & Exams, Closing, or similar), Claude stops and asks how you'd like to proceed, e.g. a consolidation note instead, or a different week, rather than generating something automatically.

Enrichment vs. mismatch

Enrichment, not flagged

An uploaded e-note covers a closely related extra sub-topic. Folded in as an extra Presentation Step, no flag needed.

Mismatch, flagged

An uploaded e-note covers an unrelated topic. Claude still teaches the scheme's topic and says so in its reply.

English Studies / English Language weeks

These list several parallel strands per week (Speech Work, Grammar, Reading & Comprehension, and so on) instead of one topic. Each strand maps to one period. English always gets 4 periods, if a week lists more than 4 strands, the two most closely related are merged into one period, and the preamble notes which ones.

How the dates are computed

  1. Claude fetches your state's official school calendar and finds the term's resumption date (Monday of Week 1).
  2. Week n Monday = Resumption Monday + (n − 1) × 7 days; Week n Friday = that Monday + 4 days.
  3. A Thursday to Friday mid-term break shortens the week to Monday to Wednesday, noted explicitly.
  4. A break covering the whole week re-maps to the next teaching week.
  5. A public holiday inside the week is noted, remaining days still get notes.
  6. The academic session (e.g. 2025/2026) is read from the fetched calendar, not guessed.

Content sourcing, by type

Content typePriority order
Definitions, examplesTextbook → e-note → online / standard textbooks
Diagrams, graphs, charts, mapsGenerated directly when rule-based and verifiable, otherwise fetched and verified from a credible online source (maps always fetched, never hand-drawn), then embedded as a real image in the document
FormulasExactly as shown in textbook or e-note, else sourced online, never altered
ExercisesAdapted from textbook or e-note first, else sourced or built from research

If neither a textbook nor an e-note was uploaded for that Subject + Class + Term at all, every content type is built entirely from credible online research.

Diagrams, graphs, charts & maps

Where a lesson genuinely needs a visual, it is embedded directly in the document rather than left for you to add:

  • Generated directly: mathematical graphs, statistical charts, geometric constructions, number lines, simple circuit/apparatus schematics, anything rule-based enough to verify.
  • Fetched and verified online: labelled biological/anatomical diagrams, apparatus photos, life cycles, historical images, anything representational rather than rule-based.
  • Maps are always fetched from a credible source, never hand-drawn, so borders, place names, and locations stay accurate.
  • A bolded, bracketed placeholder is only used as a last resort, when your Project doesn't have web search or code execution turned on, or a genuinely suitable image can't be found.

Quality control rules

  1. Clear, formal British English (UK), except Yoruba (pure Yoruba, full tonal marks) and Arabic (full Arabic script).
  2. Every step has both a Teacher's To-Do and a notebook-style Students' Note, never merged.
  3. Instructional Materials, Reference Materials, Evaluation, and Assignment are always real lists.
  4. Formulas and calculations always use proper mathematical formatting.
  5. Dates are always computed fresh from the fetched state calendar, never hardcoded.
  6. No content repeats across periods within the same week.
  7. Only the Scheme of Work entry (plus any e-note/textbook) for the exact Subject + Class + Term containing the requested week is used.
  8. A non-teaching week stops for your input rather than generating automatically.

Quick reference

  • Missing details? Claude asks instead of guessing.
  • Yoruba / Arabic subject? Generated entirely in that language's full script.
  • Not happy with a result? Just reply in the same chat, e.g. "make Step 3 shorter."
